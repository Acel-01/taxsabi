# Tax Efficiency: How to Think About Reliefs and Timing

## The one idea that drives everything

Income tax in Nigeria is charged in graduated bands, so the tax system rewards a simple piece of arithmetic: every naira of eligible deduction removes a naira of chargeable income from the top of the stack, where the marginal rate is highest. The value of a deduction is therefore the amount of the deduction multiplied by the marginal rate of the band it displaces.

Some consequences of that idea:

- In the 15% band, N100,000 of deductions saves N15,000 of tax.
- In the 18% band, N100,000 saves N18,000.
- In the 21% band, N100,000 saves N21,000.
- In the 23% band, N100,000 saves N23,000.
- In the 25% band, N100,000 saves N25,000.

The same deduction is worth more to a higher earner because it displaces income that would otherwise be taxed at a higher rate. This is not a loophole; it is how a progressive band structure behaves. The planning task is to know which band the individual is in, and to make sure every eligible deduction they are entitled to is actually claimed and documented.

## Know the bands before planning anything

The 2026 bands, with their cumulative boundaries:

| Cumulative income | Band | Rate |
|---|---|---|
| Up to N800,000 | First | 0% |
| N800,000 - N3,000,000 | Next N2,200,000 | 15% |
| N3,000,000 - N12,000,000 | Next N9,000,000 | 18% |
| N12,000,000 - N25,000,000 | Next N13,000,000 | 21% |
| N25,000,000 - N50,000,000 | Next N25,000,000 | 23% |
| Above N50,000,000 | Balance | 25% |

The first question in any tax-planning conversation is: what is the projected chargeable income, and which band does the marginal naira sit in? The answer determines what each additional naira of deduction is worth.

## The main reliefs, ranked by how much control the employee has

**Voluntary pension contributions — the flexible lever.** Of all the eligible deductions, the individual's pension contribution is the one most within the employee's control. An employee in the Contributory Pension Scheme may make voluntary contributions to their Retirement Savings Account in addition to the mandatory contributions made by themselves and their employer. The mechanics matter: the voluntary contribution must be from legitimate income and must not exceed one-third of the month's salary, contributions flow through the employer into the Retirement Savings Account, and the frequency is limited to once a month. Half of each voluntary contribution is treated as contingent — available for withdrawal — and half is fixed for pension until retirement, with a minimum two-year retention before any contingent withdrawal. The tax rule that keeps this honest: income earned on voluntary contributions is subject to tax at the point of withdrawal if the withdrawal happens before five years from the date of contribution.

That combination — deductible when contributed, tax-deferred on the income if left for five years, and tax-free retirement benefits at the end — makes voluntary pension contributions the most tax-efficient flexible savings vehicle in the framework. It is also money genuinely locked toward retirement, so the amount must be sized to the employee's cash needs, not merely to the tax saving.

**Rent relief — document what you pay.** Rent relief is 20% of annual rent paid, capped at N500,000. The employee cannot choose the rent, but can make sure it is accurately declared and supported, so the deduction is actually granted.

**Mortgage interest — tied to the loan.** Interest on a loan for developing an owner-occupied residential house is deductible. The amount is a matter of the loan and the interest paid; the planning task is record-keeping.

**Life insurance and annuity premiums — contractual.** The annual premium or annuity amount paid in the year preceding the year of assessment, on the individual's life or a spouse's life, is deductible. The planning task is to know the contract and keep the receipts.

**NHF and NHIS contributions — scheme-based.** Contributions under the National Housing Fund and the National Health Insurance Scheme are deductible. Where an individual contributes, the contribution should be captured in claims and evidence.

## Worked comparisons

**A middle-band saver.** An employee with chargeable income of N3,000,000 owes tax of N330,000 (N800,000 at 0% plus N2,200,000 at 15%). If they make a voluntary pension contribution of N150,000, chargeable income falls to N2,850,000, and the tax becomes N307,500 — a saving of N22,500, which is exactly 15% of N150,000. The saving arises because the contribution reduced income that would have been taxed at 15%.

**A rent-relief household.** An employee with chargeable income of N5,000,000 pays N330,000 plus 18% of N2,000,000 — N690,000 in total. If they claim rent relief of N480,000 (20% of N2,400,000 rent), chargeable income falls to N4,520,000 and the tax falls to N603,600. The saving is N86,400 — 18% of the deduction, because the deduction displaced income taxed at the 18% band.

**A high earner in the top band.** An individual with chargeable income of N50,000,000 pays N10,430,000 in tax: N330,000 from the 15% band, N1,620,000 from the 18% band, N2,730,000 from the 21% band, and N5,750,000 from the 23% band. Every additional N100,000 of qualifying deduction — for example a voluntary pension contribution — saves N25,000, because the next naira would otherwise sit above the N50,000,000 threshold at the 25% rate. A deduction of N1,000,000 in this position saves N250,000.

The pattern to notice in all three examples: the tax saving is the deduction multiplied by the rate of the band the income was removed from. Nothing more, nothing less.

## Sequencing through the year

Tax planning for an employee is a year-long exercise, not a December event:

1. **Estimate the year's income early.** Salary, allowable allowances, and any other income build the base. The band that the projected income reaches determines the marginal rate for planning.
2. **Confirm the deductions that are facts.** Rent paid, mortgage interest, insurance premiums, NHF and NHIS contributions — these are observable during the year. Records should be assembled as they occur.
3. **Decide the flexible deduction.** Voluntary pension contributions can be adjusted month by month, subject to the one-third-of-salary cap and the monthly frequency limit. Sizing them with the marginal rate in mind converts taxable income into retirement savings at a discount equal to the rate deferred.
4. **Claim in writing, with evidence.** No deduction exists without the written claim in the prescribed form, and the authority may demand documentary evidence for any of them.
5. **Reconcile with PAYE.** The employer deducts tax monthly from salary and must ensure the year's total deductions equal the employee's income tax for the year. Reliefs claimed during the year affect what the payroll should deduct; reliefs claimed late are sorted out through the return process, not silently.

## What tax efficiency is not

- It is not a reason to inflate rent or invent payments. Rent relief requires an accurate declaration of actual rent paid; evidence that is absent or inadequate can cause the authority to refuse the deduction or part of it.
- It is not free money. A voluntary pension contribution still moves the employee's own cash into a retirement account with access conditions: half is fixed until retirement, contingent withdrawals have a two-year maturity, and withdrawals within five years of a contribution can attract tax on the income earned.
- It is not the same for everyone. The minimum wage earner is outside the chargeable-income computation altogether. For them, the planning conversation is about records and compliance, not about deductions: there is no tax to reduce.
- It is not a substitute for professional advice on unusual situations — foreign income, business income, or benefits in kind all raise questions that a relief guide does not answer.

## A final word on discipline

The framework rewards organisation. The deductions are known, the bands are published, the evidence requirements are stated. An employee who tracks rent, pension statements, insurance receipts, and scheme contributions during the year will claim more of what they are entitled to than one who reconstructs records after the fact. Tax efficiency in this system is less about clever manoeuvres and more about knowing the list, keeping the paper, and using the flexible lever with the marginal rate in view.
