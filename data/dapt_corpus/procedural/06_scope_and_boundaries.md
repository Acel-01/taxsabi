# Scope and Boundaries: What the 2026 Framework Covers

## The operative law

The 2026 year of assessment begins on 1 January 2026. For individual income tax, the governing statute is the Nigeria Tax Act 2025, which applies throughout Nigeria to any person required to comply with its provisions, whether personally or on behalf of another person. Tax administration — returns, assessments, remittance obligations, and penalties — is governed by the Nigeria Tax Administration Act 2025.

Personal income tax is administered by the relevant tax authority, in line with the federal structure. Employers deduct PAYE from employment income and remit it to the relevant state tax authority; the Lagos State Internal Revenue Service is the reference example of a state authority publishing guidance for employers and employees. The procedural framework, however, is national: the same Act defines the bands, the deductions, and the compliance duties across the country.

## What is different in 2026

Anyone working from older Nigerian tax material should unlearn several things:

- **The personal relief system has changed.** There is no consolidated relief allowance under the current Act. The Act contains no personal relief or consolidated relief allowance concept. Relief for individuals now comes from the zero-rate band — the first N800,000 of chargeable income at 0% — and the eligible deductions in section 30.
- **The rate table is new.** The graduated bands run from 0% to 25% across six steps, with cumulative boundaries at N800,000, N3,000,000, N12,000,000, N25,000,000, and N50,000,000.
- **Minimum wage earners are exempt.** An individual earning the national minimum wage — not less than N70,000 per month under the 2024 amendment to the National Minimum Wage Act — is outside the chargeable-income computation.
- **Old guidance is not harmless.** A payroll sheet that applies a consolidated relief allowance, or a set of talking points built on the repealed Personal Income Tax Act, will produce wrong answers with confidence. The first line of defence is knowing which statute is in force.

## What this material covers

The material in this corpus is about personal income tax for individuals, with a focus on employees and the employment relationship:

- How individual income tax is computed for the 2026 year: total income, eligible deductions, chargeable income, and the graduated bands.
- The eligible deductions: NHF, NHIS, and pension contributions; mortgage interest on an owner-occupied home; qualifying life insurance and annuity premiums; and rent relief.
- The claim and evidence discipline that turns an eligible deduction into an actual one.
- PAYE administration: employer withholding and remittance, monthly and annual returns, and the employee's annual return.
- Pensions: the Contributory Pension Scheme, mandatory contributions, voluntary contributions, remittance deadlines, and the tax treatment of contributions, fund income, and benefits.
- Tax planning logic for employees: marginal rates, the value of deductions, and the voluntary pension contribution as the flexible lever.

## What this material does not cover

- Companies and other business entities, and the taxes that apply to them.
- Business income, trading profits, and the rules for the self-employed beyond the basic framework.
- Consumption and transaction taxes such as value added tax and withholding tax, which have their own statutes, deadlines, and penalties. Where a deadline such as the 21st of the following month appears in the general deduction-at-source penalty framework, it belongs to that family of obligations, not to PAYE.
- Capital gains, stamp duties, and sector-specific levies.
- Cross-border situations, foreign income, and treaty questions.
- Individual circumstances that turn on facts a guide cannot know: benefits in kind, multiple employers, unusual allowances, disputes with a tax authority.

Where a question falls outside this scope, the honest answer is that it needs the applicable statute or a professional adviser — not an extrapolation from the rules summarized here.

## The discipline behind every statement

Three habits keep tax guidance trustworthy, and they are the habits this corpus is built on:

1. **Trace every fact to a source.** The bands, deductions, deadlines, and rates in these documents come from the Nigeria Tax Act 2025, the Nigeria Tax Administration Act 2025, the Pension Reform Act 2014, the National Minimum Wage Act as amended in 2024, and regulator guidance from the National Pension Commission and state tax authorities. Facts are catalogued with their verification status before they are used.
2. **Distinguish the primary text from commentary.** Where a statute says something precisely, quote it precisely. Where the operating detail lives in regulations or guidelines, say so.
3. **Say what is not known.** A pending fact is labelled pending and kept out of the material until it is verified. Confident-sounding invention is the failure mode that damages trust in tax guidance more than any other — and in Nigeria's 2026 framework, where the rules genuinely changed, the risk of recycling superseded guidance is real.

## A closing note on the 2026 transition

The transition to the 2025 Acts is the biggest change to Nigerian personal income tax in years. For employees, the practical differences are concrete: a zero-rate first band, a short list of eligible deductions, no consolidated relief allowance, a minimum wage exemption, and a clear split between PAYE's monthly rhythm and pension's seven-working-day clock. For anyone advising, preparing payroll, or planning personal finances, the work starts with the same step: confirm the framework, then apply it to the facts — with the evidence to match.
