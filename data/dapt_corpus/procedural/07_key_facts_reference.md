# Key Facts Reference

A condensed reference of the verified facts behind the 2026 Nigerian tax framework. Plain statements only, no questions or answers.

## The framework

The Nigeria Tax Act 2025 applies throughout Nigeria. The 2026 year of assessment begins on 1 January 2026. The Act contains no consolidated relief allowance and no personal relief concept; individual relief comes from the zero-rate band and the section 30 deductions. An individual earning the national minimum wage — N70,000 per month under the National Minimum Wage Act as amended in 2024 — is exempt from income tax on their chargeable income.

## The tax bands

The Fourth Schedule bands apply to chargeable income: the first N800,000 at 0%; the next N2,200,000 at 15%, reaching a cumulative N3,000,000; the next N9,000,000 at 18%, reaching a cumulative N12,000,000; the next N13,000,000 at 21%, reaching a cumulative N25,000,000; the next N25,000,000 at 23%, reaching a cumulative N50,000,000; and amounts above N50,000,000 at 25%.

## Eligible deductions

The deductions available to individuals under section 30(2)(a) are: contributions under the National Housing Fund; contributions under the National Health Insurance Scheme; contributions under the Pension Reform Act; interest on loans for developing an owner-occupied residential house; life insurance or deferred annuity premiums on the individual's life or a spouse's life; and rent relief. Rent relief is 20% of annual rent paid, subject to a maximum of N500,000, whichever is lower, and the taxpayer must accurately declare the actual rent paid. A deduction is allowed only if claimed in writing in the prescribed form, and the tax authority may require documentary evidence; where evidence is absent or inadequate, the deduction or part of it may be refused. In filing practice, evidence of pension, life assurance, NHIS, and NHF contributions is mandatory for those reliefs to be granted.

## Employment and PAYE

The employer deducts income tax at source from emoluments and must ensure the aggregate of deductions over the year equals the employee's income tax for that year. PAYE deducted from salaries is remitted to the relevant state internal revenue service by the 10th day of the following month. Employers file an annual return by 31 January covering all employees of the preceding year, disclosing gross emoluments (including allowances and benefits in kind), total deductions, net emoluments, and tax deducted per employee. Monthly returns are required for tax deducted at source, and employees file annual returns of income from all sources. The general penalty framework for failure to remit deducted tax includes the unremitted amount, an administrative penalty of 10% per annum, and interest at the prevailing Central Bank of Nigeria Monetary Policy Rate.

## Pensions

Mandatory pension contributions are a minimum of 10% of monthly emoluments by the employer and a minimum of 8% by the employee. The employer deducts the employee's contribution at source and remits both contributions to the Pension Fund Custodian not later than seven working days from the day the employee is paid. Failure to deduct or remit within that window attracts a penalty of not less than 2% of the unpaid contribution for each month or part of a month the default continues, recoverable as a debt owed to the employee's retirement savings account.

## Voluntary pension contributions

An eligible employee may make voluntary pension contributions in addition to the mandatory amounts. Voluntary contributions are made only in Naira, through the employer, into the employee's retirement savings account; the employee notifies the employer in writing of the intention and the amount. A voluntary contribution must come from legitimate income and must not exceed one-third of the month's salary, and the frequency is limited to once a month. For active contributors, 50% of each voluntary contribution is contingent and 50% is fixed until retirement; the contribution must be retained for at least two years before access, and withdrawals are permitted once every two years on incremental contributions.

## Pension tax treatment

Pension contributions are deductible in computing chargeable income. Income accruable to pension funds and assets is not taxable, and retirement benefits payable under the Pension Reform Act are not taxable. Income earned on a voluntary contribution is subject to tax at the point of withdrawal if the withdrawal is made before the end of five years from the date the contribution was made.

## Refunds, assessments, and objections

Overpaid tax is refunded after an audit by the relevant tax authority. Refunds are to be made within 90 days of the authority's decision, with the option of a set-off against any tax liability, and no refund claim is allowed unless made in writing within six years after the end of the year of assessment to which it relates. Excess credits such as withholding tax credits may be applied against future liabilities or refunded, subject to supporting documentation. A refund obtained through a false or fictitious claim attracts recovery of the amount, a penalty of 50% of that amount, and interest at the prevailing Central Bank of Nigeria Monetary Policy Rate. A taxpayer disputing an assessment must deliver a written notice of objection within 30 days of service of the notice of assessment. The tax authority may raise additional assessments within six years of an assessment.
